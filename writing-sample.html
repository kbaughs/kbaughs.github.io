Escalating a Priority 1 Support Ticket

Version 2.1  ·  Last updated October 2026  ·  Owner: Support Operations

Writing sample by Kaela Baughs  ·  kbaughs.github.io  ·  baughsk@gmail.com

Sample document. Fernhollow Software is a fictional company, and every name, tool and number below is invented.

Overview

This procedure explains how a Support Specialist identifies a Priority 1 (P1) ticket, escalates it to the on-call engineer, and keeps the customer updated until the issue is resolved.

Purpose

A P1 means a customer cannot use Fernhollow to run their business. Every minute counts, and the customer needs to know we are on it. Following these steps makes sure the right engineer is working on the issue as fast as possible, nothing is lost in the handoff, and the customer hears from us on a predictable schedule.

Scope

  • Support Specialists (Tier 1)
  • On-call Tier 2 Engineers
  • Support Lead on duty

At a glance

First reply to customer
Within 15 minutes of the ticket arriving
Escalate to Tier 2
Within 30 minutes of confirming the P1
Customer updates
Every 60 minutes until resolved
Incident summary
Within 1 business day of resolution

1 Confirm the ticket is a P1

A ticket is a P1 if at least one of the following is true and there is no workaround:

  • Fernhollow is down or unusable for all users on the customer's account.
  • Customer data is missing, corrupted or visible to people who shouldn't see it.
  • Payments or invoicing are failing for all of the customer's own customers.

These are not P1s. Handle them as Priority 2 using the standard ticket process:

  • An issue affecting one user, or a few users, on the account
  • Fernhollow is slow, but still working
  • A problem that has a workaround the customer can use for now

When in doubt, escalate. Downgrading a ticket later takes seconds. A missed P1 can cost us the customer.

2 Send the first reply

Reply to the customer within 15 minutes, even before you have answers. Use this template:

Template: P1 first reply

Hi [first name],

Thank you for letting us know. We've marked this as our highest priority, and an engineer is being brought in now. I'll update you within the hour, or sooner if we learn more.

If anything changes on your end, reply here and it will reach me right away.

[Your name], Fernhollow Support

3 Gather the details

Collect the following before you escalate. If the customer hasn't provided something, ask for it in your first reply, but don't hold the escalation waiting for it.

  1. Account name and plan
  2. What is affected, and how many users
  3. When the issue started
  4. Steps to reproduce, if known
  5. Error messages or screenshots
  6. What you or the customer have already tried
  7. The best contact for updates, and their time zone

4 Escalate to Tier 2

  1. In the ticket, set Priority to P1.
  2. Assign the ticket to the Tier 2: On Call queue.
  3. Post in the #p1-escalations channel using the template below.
  4. If no engineer acknowledges your post within 10 minutes, page the on-call engineer from the on-call schedule.
    1. If there is still no response after another 10 minutes, page the Support Lead on duty.

Template: #p1-escalations post

P1: [account name]

Ticket: [ticket link]
Impact: [what is affected, and how many users]
Started: [time and time zone]
Tried so far: [steps already taken]
Customer contact: [name, time zone]

Enterprise plan only Also tag the account's Customer Success Manager in your post. They'll decide whether to contact the customer's leadership directly.

5 Keep the customer updated

Send an update every 60 minutes until the issue is resolved, even if nothing has changed. Silence feels worse to a customer than "we're still working on it."

Template: P1 hourly update

Hi [first name],

Here's where we are:

  • What we're working on: [current step]
  • What we've ruled out: [causes checked]
  • Next update: by [time and time zone]

[Your name], Fernhollow Support

6 Close out the P1

  1. When Tier 2 confirms the fix, ask the customer to confirm everything works on their end.
  2. Only after the customer confirms, set the ticket status to Resolved.
  3. Within 1 business day, complete the Incident Summary form: what happened, how long it lasted, the cause and what prevents it from happening again.
  4. Send the summary to the customer, and link it in the ticket.

Exceptions

The customer calls something a P1 that isn't
Reply within 15 minutes anyway. Explain the priority you've set and why, and when they can expect a fix. Never dismiss the issue; it is urgent to them.
Several tickets arrive for the same P1
Keep the first ticket open. Merge the others into it, and reply to each person so they know which ticket to follow.
The issue involves data being visible to the wrong people
Escalate as normal, and also message the Security Lead directly. Don't post account details or data in #p1-escalations; write "security issue, details sent to Security Lead" instead.
The P1 arrives outside business hours
The after-hours specialist follows this procedure as written. Page the on-call engineer immediately instead of waiting 10 minutes for an acknowledgment.
